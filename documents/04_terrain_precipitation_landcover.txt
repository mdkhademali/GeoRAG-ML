Terrain, Precipitation and Land-Cover Reference Datasets

SRTM elevation. The Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (Farr and colleagues, 2007) produced near-global elevation data in February 2000. The 1 arc-second product, about 30 m, is available on Earth Engine as USGS/SRTMGL1_003. SRTM measures the surface including vegetation canopy and buildings to some extent, so it is closer to a surface model than a bare-earth terrain model. Slope is derived from elevation. In low-relief floodplains such as much of Bangladesh, vertical error can be comparable to real elevation differences, so elevation and slope carry limited information there.

CHIRPS precipitation. Climate Hazards Group InfraRed Precipitation with Station data (Funk and colleagues, 2015) blends satellite infrared estimates with rain-gauge data. It is quasi-global, available from 1981, at 0.05 degree resolution, which is roughly 5.5 km. The Earth Engine daily collection is UCSB-CHG/CHIRPS/DAILY. Because the native resolution is much coarser than Landsat or Sentinel-2, CHIRPS-derived features appear as large blocks and add little pixel-level discrimination. Their main value is regional context.

ESA WorldCover. WorldCover provides a 10 m global land-cover map derived from Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2, with versions for 2020 (v100) and 2021 (v200). Earth Engine collections are ESA/WorldCover/v100 and ESA/WorldCover/v200, band Map. Class codes: 10 tree cover, 20 shrubland, 30 grassland, 40 cropland, 50 built-up, 60 bare or sparse vegetation, 70 snow and ice, 80 permanent water bodies, 90 herbaceous wetland, 95 mangroves, 100 moss and lichen. Product validation reports overall accuracy in the mid-70 percent range globally, and accuracy varies by class and region. WorldCover is a single-epoch product in this workflow, so it is used as a reference layer and not as evidence of land-cover change between 2019 and 2025.
